Track the rules that affect what a character can do and what it costs. A rule belongs in the record when breaking it would change a scene’s outcome. The simplest way to begin is to identify the scene that creates the expectation and the later scene that must honor, change, or resolve it. That gives you a manageable editorial problem instead of an anxious instruction to “check everything.”

For a rule, its boundary, its cost, and its consequence, the standard is not perfect documentation. It is reliable cause and effect. If healers can close a wound in minutes but cannot restore nerve damage, that limit should shape who travels, who fights, and which injuries leave a mark after the scene ends. If a reader would reasonably carry that information forward, you need a way to carry it forward too.

## Start with the scene that creates the expectation

Find the earliest scene that makes the relevant fact meaningful. Record what the reader is likely to infer, what remains uncertain, and what later scene depends on that inference. This prevents a late repair from accidentally preserving an intention that never reached the page.

The pressure to protect is the promise that the world will remain coherent while characters test its limits. That pressure helps you distinguish a real issue from a harmless variation in wording or detail.

## Record the change, not just the fact

A useful note has a before state, a trigger, and an after state. In this case, a rule matters when an exception exposes its price or political meaning. That structure tells you what must be visible if the book is going to ask the reader to accept the new condition.

Keep the note tied to a chapter or scene. A precise reference is more useful than a polished summary because it lets you verify the language when another revision changes the context.

## Repair the first broken handoff

When you find a mismatch, trace it back to the first moment where the chain stops making sense. a convenient exception that removes the very obstacle a scene has asked the reader to take seriously The best repair may belong in the setup, the transition, or the consequence rather than in the final scene where the problem becomes obvious.

Choose whether an exception deepens the rule or quietly cancels it only after rereading the sequence around it. A repair should preserve the reader’s understanding from one scene to the next, not merely make two isolated statements agree.

## Keep the review light enough to repeat

Use a rulebook arranged around constraints and consequences during major revision, and update it when you move, combine, or delete scenes. You do not need an elaborate database; you need a short list that is easier to consult than another complete reread.

If you want a longer workflow, read [How to Build a Worldbuilding Rulebook That Helps You Write](/blog/how-to-build-a-worldbuilding-rulebook-that-helps-you-write). It explains how to turn the same check into a calm revision pass without losing the story’s texture.

## The useful final question

Before you move on, ask whether the current draft gives the reader enough on-page cause to accept the outcome. If the answer is yes, the detail is doing its job. If it is no, return to the earliest scene that made the later outcome matter and revise the connection there.
