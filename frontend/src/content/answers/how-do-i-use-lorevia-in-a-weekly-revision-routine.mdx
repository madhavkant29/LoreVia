Build the week around one revision lens, not around using every feature. On the first day, define the editorial question. Next, gather and verify chapter evidence. Spend the middle of the week editing a bounded set of scenes. Finish by checking consequences, recording decisions, and saving a clearly named manuscript version.

## Day one: choose the lens

Select a pass that can produce finished changes in one week: one character arc, one relationship, a date-sensitive sequence, a set of dropped threads, or the setup for the climax. Avoid “improve the whole novel.”

Write a completion statement. For example: “By Friday, every scene involving Kira’s injured shoulder will follow the chosen recovery timeline,” or “I will clarify the three decisions that move Kira and Sol from alliance to mistrust.”

Confirm that LoreVia contains the draft you are reviewing. Note its version and chapter count. If your local manuscript has moved ahead, decide whether to process the newer stable version before the pass.

## Day two: gather evidence

Use the view that fits the question. Follow a character across chapters, inspect relationship changes, trace events on the timeline, or ask a bounded advisor question. Collect chapter references and open them in context.

For each finding, record:

- what the manuscript appears to establish;
- the relevant chapter or scene;
- whether the evidence is confirmed or ambiguous;
- the desired canon or reader effect;
- which other scenes may depend on it.

Stop gathering when you have enough evidence to decide. Research can become avoidance if it does not narrow the edit.

<Callout title="Protect the writing days">

Set a limit on discovery. A short, verified queue is more valuable than a large report you cannot finish.

</Callout>

## Days three and four: revise in batches

Order edits by dependency. Change the establishing scene first, then the scenes that react to it. Keep one issue together even if it spans several chapters; context switching increases the chance of creating new contradictions.

Imagine the pass concerns a secret map. Chapter four establishes that Kira cannot read it, chapter eight has her identify a route, and chapter eleven finally shows Sol teaching her the symbols. You could move or revise the teaching scene, seed partial knowledge earlier, or change the chapter-eight action. Once you choose, inspect every later use of the map.

Mark an item complete only when the manuscript is edited, not when the problem is understood. Add new discoveries to a parking list unless they block the current pass.

## Day five: verify consequences

Read the changed scenes in narrative order. Then inspect the scene before and after each one. Check names, time, location, knowledge, emotional state, and objects that travel through the edit.

Ask the original question again against the revised manuscript manually, or process a new version after the batch is stable. Do not expect analysis of the old upload to reflect local changes.

Update the story bible or decision log with the chosen canon. Name the new file clearly and preserve the earlier version until the pass is accepted.

## Keep the routine small

A workable weekly board can have four columns: investigate, verified, editing, checked. Limit “editing” to one or two items. This makes unfinished work visible and discourages starting a new thread whenever revision becomes difficult.

At the end of the week, note one sentence about what improved and one about what remains uncertain. Carry forward only a few high-priority items.

If you have less time, compress the rhythm into three sessions: question and evidence, edits, verification. Keep the sequence intact.

## Common mistakes

Do not treat feature exploration as progress. Open a tool because it answers the week’s question.

Do not copy every possible issue into the active queue. Rank by impact and dependency.

Do not revise from an answer without reading its cited scenes.

Do not mix analysis from one draft with edits in another without labelling both.

Do not skip the verification day. Structural edits frequently alter information flow, timing, and emotional continuity outside the changed paragraph.

Do not use the routine as an inflexible productivity test. Some discoveries require a second week. Preserve quality by reducing scope, not by rushing judgment.

## A sustainable result

The routine works when each week ends with a coherent, checked improvement to the manuscript and a clearer record of canon. LoreVia’s role is to make evidence easier to retrieve and compare. The author’s role is to decide, revise, and reread.

## Before the next week

Archive the finished queue and choose the next lens from the manuscript’s needs, not from unfinished curiosity. Carry forward only findings whose delay would create new errors. A clean boundary makes progress visible and keeps last week’s analysis from becoming permanent background noise.
