You can create the foundation of a story bible automatically by extracting facts from the current manuscript. The useful result is not a finished encyclopedia. It is a searchable first pass that points back to the chapters where characters, events, relationships, locations, and rules appear. You then verify the important facts and decide what counts as canon.

## Prepare the right manuscript

Automation magnifies version confusion. Before processing anything, choose the draft you are actually revising. Give it a clear version name and date. Remove duplicate chapters, abandoned alternate scenes, editorial notes that should not be interpreted as story, and front matter that does not belong to the narrative.

Keep chapter headings consistent where possible. If the book uses dates, viewpoint labels, letters, or journal entries, preserve them because they carry structural information.

Do not clean the prose merely to satisfy a tool. A draft can be rough. The goal is to know which text produced the extracted record.

## Extract a useful first layer

Start with facts that create obligations:

- character names, aliases, roles, and relationships;
- chapter and scene structure;
- locations and movements between them;
- dated or sequenced events;
- possessions, injuries, secrets, promises, and goals;
- rules governing the world, institutions, or magic;
- unresolved questions and planted details.

An extraction may include errors. A title can be mistaken for a name. Two aliases can become separate people. A hypothetical statement can appear factual. That is normal. The value lies in narrowing thousands of sentences into records you can inspect.

<Callout title="Automatic does not mean authoritative">

Let software build the index. Let the manuscript supply the evidence. Let the author decide canon.

</Callout>

## Verify by consequence

Do not review every item with equal intensity. Begin with facts that would damage multiple scenes if wrong: identity, chronology, family ties, deaths, abilities, major relationships, and world rules.

Open the cited chapter. Read enough context to distinguish narration from dialogue, truth from lie, memory from present action, and plan from completed event. Mark the record verified, uncertain, intentionally contradictory, or incorrect.

Suppose an extraction says that Mina is Rowan’s sister. The cited line is spoken by an antagonist who is attempting to provoke him. A later family record contradicts it. The bible should not flatten both statements into one fact. Record the claim, its speaker, and the confirmed relationship separately.

## Merge identities and normalize names

Characters often appear under surnames, titles, nicknames, disguises, and pronouns. Merge aliases only after confirming they refer to the same entity. Preserve the aliases as searchable terms.

Normalize locations and objects as well. “North Gate,” “the northern gate,” and “Gate Three” may refer to one place—or to different entrances. Avoid merging because labels look similar.

Decide simple conventions for dates, status labels, and relationship direction. Consistency makes later comparison possible.

## Add editorial meaning

Extraction tells you that a detail appears. A maintained bible should also tell you why it matters. Add fields such as consequence, current status, unresolved question, last verified chapter, and next scene to inspect.

For a character relationship, record not only “allies” but the change that produced the state: reluctant cooperation after the bridge collapse, trust weakened by the concealed letter, or public loyalty with private suspicion.

For a world rule, preserve limits and exceptions. “Healing requires salt” is incomplete if chapter twelve establishes that fresh wounds close without it during an eclipse.

## Keep the bible alive during revision

After structural edits, refresh affected chapters rather than assuming the original extraction remains accurate. Mark deleted facts retired instead of erasing their history when they may explain old notes. Record the manuscript version used for each review.

Adopt a small weekly routine:

1. update scenes revised that week;
2. resolve new aliases and duplicates;
3. verify high-impact uncertain facts;
4. close or carry forward open threads;
5. export or back up the current canon.

## Common mistakes

Do not ask automation to invent missing backstory and store it beside extracted facts. Separate on-page canon, author plans, and suggestions clearly.

Do not turn every noun into an entry. A story bible becomes unusable when trivial facts bury consequential ones.

Do not accept summaries without evidence links. When a record affects an edit, you need to find its source quickly.

Do not treat one successful extraction as permanent. The manuscript will change; the bible must follow.

## The practical goal

Automatic creation should save the labour of initial indexing while preserving the author’s judgment. Aim for a verified, searchable map that helps you answer questions and find passages—not a decorative document that claims certainty it has not earned.

## Before you finish

Test the new bible with three questions you genuinely struggled to answer while revising. If it returns a source, preserves uncertainty, and shows the current canon quickly, the automatic first pass has become a useful author tool.
